# Systems and service design
When effects, handoffs, institutions or dependencies extend beyond screens.

## Concepts and application cues

- **System boundaries:** Define what is inside and outside the analysis, whose perspective determines that boundary, and what is lost by excluding elements.
- **Stocks and flows:** Distinguish an accumulated quantity from its rates of increase and decrease; identify units and delays.
- **Feedback loops:** Trace causal chains that reinforce change or counteract it; check direction, timing and evidence.
- **Leverage points:** Consider changing information, rules, goals, or system structure, not only numerical parameters; assess feasibility and power.
- **Second-order consequences:** Trace plausible downstream effects and who bears costs; label speculation.
- **Stakeholder / ecosystem mapping:** Identify actors, relationships, resources, incentives and decision authority.
- **Service blueprint:** Map user actions, visible staff or interface actions, backstage activities and support processes along a time axis; clarify handoffs and failures.
- **5E experience framework:** Explore entice, enter, engage, exit and extend as one useful service-experience lens; variants exist.
- **Resilience / local vs global optimization:** Explore failures, recovery, bottlenecks and whether optimizing one part harms the whole.

## Fictional example
A library lending kiosk may streamline pickup while creating a new staff queue. Map both visitor and staff actions and test the handoff.

## Limits and misuse
A map is a hypothesis about a system, not evidence that the relationships exist. Avoid adding unnecessary actors or complexity.

## Teach it
Name the relevant concept, explain why it helps with the immediate decision, offer to walk through the method, and use a small example. Ask what would count as useful completion. Do not assign a framework merely because it exists.
