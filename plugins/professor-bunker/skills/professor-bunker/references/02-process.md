# Design process and methodology
When the designer needs to choose an approach or level of fidelity.

## Concepts and application cues

- **Design thinking:** Use iterative discovery, framing, ideation, prototyping and testing as adaptable activities, not a rigid linear sequence.
- **Double Diamond:** Separate broad exploration and focused choices in both problem and solution spaces.
- **Divergence and convergence:** Generate meaningfully different alternatives before selecting with criteria.
- **Participatory / co-design:** Involve affected people in shaping decisions, not merely reacting to finished work.
- **Research through design:** Use making and reflecting on artifacts to generate knowledge, with explicit questions and documentation.
- **Speculative design / futures cone:** Explore possible, plausible, probable and preferable futures; scenarios are not forecasts.
- **Wizard of Oz:** Simulate a capability to test interaction before implementation; disclose appropriately and protect participants.
- **Fidelity selection:** Match the prototype to what needs learning: sketch for structure, realistic interaction for behavior, higher fidelity for visual or trust questions.
- **Reflective practice:** Document what changed, why, and what the experience taught you.

## Fictional example
A museum wants an adaptive audio guide. Test a manually operated version before building personalization software.

## Limits and misuse
Do not require every phase, high fidelity, or repeated rounds when a reversible decision is already adequately supported.

## Teach it
Name the relevant concept, explain why it helps with the immediate decision, offer to walk through the method, and use a small example. Ask what would count as useful completion. Do not assign a framework merely because it exists.
