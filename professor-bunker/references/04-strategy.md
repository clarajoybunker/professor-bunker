# Product strategy and business
When value, adoption, incentives or sustainability are uncertain.

## Concepts and application cues

- **Business Model Canvas:** Explore customer segments, value propositions, channels, relationships, revenue, resources, activities, partners and costs as hypotheses.
- **Value proposition / Jobs to Be Done:** Describe the progress a person seeks in a specific situation, and how alternatives satisfy that need.
- **Desirability, feasibility, viability:** Evaluate human value, practical implementation and sustainable delivery separately; include ethics and accessibility rather than treating them as optional.
- **Incentives:** Compare who pays, benefits, decides, does the work and bears risk.
- **Product-market fit:** Look for converging behavioral evidence of demand and sustained value, not a universal threshold.
- **Competitive alternatives / switching costs:** Consider existing workarounds, doing nothing, migration effort and social or institutional constraints.
- **Network effects / cold start:** Ask whether additional participants increase value, and how useful the service is before the network exists.
- **Adoption / retention:** Separate initial trial from repeated meaningful use, accounting for natural usage frequency.
- **Unit economics:** Estimate per-unit revenue and relevant variable costs; declare assumptions and avoid invented financial figures.

## Fictional example
A fictional neighborhood repair exchange needs enough repairers before it attracts customers. Explore a standalone booking tool as a lower-dependency starting point.

## Limits and misuse
Do not assume every public or nonprofit service needs monetization, or mistake stated enthusiasm for validated demand.

## Teach it
Name the relevant concept, explain why it helps with the immediate decision, offer to walk through the method, and use a small example. Ask what would count as useful completion. Do not assign a framework merely because it exists.
