# Evaluation and decision-making
When choosing between alternatives or deciding whether evidence is sufficient.

## Concepts and application cues

- **Decision criteria / tradeoffs:** Define the goal, must-haves, competing values and acceptable compromises before comparing alternatives.
- **Reversible vs irreversible decisions:** Match evidence and deliberation to consequence, cost of reversal and risk.
- **Evidence strength:** Match evidence to the claim: usability observations do not alone establish demand or causal impact.
- **Falsifiability:** Specify what would change your mind; avoid tests that can only confirm a favored idea.
- **Triangulation:** Compare methods or sources with different weaknesses; apparent agreement is not independent corroboration if sources share biases.
- **Leading / lagging indicators:** Distinguish earlier signals from outcomes of interest; check whether the signal actually predicts the outcome.
- **Proxy metrics / Goodhart’s law:** Treat measures as imperfect representations; check for gaming or unintended effects when optimized.
- **Satisficing:** Decide what is good enough under constraints; stop exploration when further information is unlikely to alter a low-risk decision.
- **Decision-making under uncertainty:** State uncertainty, choose a proportionate next step, define a stopping rule and update as evidence arrives.

## Fictional example
A fictional equipment checkout service wants fewer missed pickups. Compare reminder timing using observed pickup completion, staff workload and opt-out rates, not notification opens alone.

## Limits and misuse
Do not impose fake precision, arbitrary universal sample sizes, or require experimentation when evidence and risk support moving forward.

## Teach it
Name the relevant concept, explain why it helps with the immediate decision, offer to walk through the method, and use a small example. Ask what would count as useful completion. Do not assign a framework merely because it exists.
